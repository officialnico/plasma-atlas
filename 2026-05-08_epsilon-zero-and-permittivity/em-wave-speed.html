Why c = 1 / √(ε₀ μ₀)

An interactive walkthrough — from wiggling a charge to the speed of light

c = 1 / √(ε₀ · μ₀) ≈ 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s
The speed of light, derived from two electromagnetic constants of the vacuum

1The leapfrog: E and B keep creating each other

Maxwell's two key equations are Faraday's law (a changing B-field makes an E-field) and the Ampère-Maxwell law (a changing E-field makes a B-field). Once a charge wiggles and starts a disturbance, these two laws bootstrap the wave forward. Click a charge below to see it happen:

The charge wiggles, an E-field ripples out, that ripple's change creates a B-field beside it, that B-field's change creates an E-field further out, and so on — forever, at the speed of light.

3Where does the speed come from?

Combining the two Maxwell equations mathematically gives a wave equation for E (and another for B):

∂²E/∂t² = (1/ε₀μ₀) · ∂²E/∂x²

Any equation of the form ∂²f/∂t² = v² · ∂²f/∂x² describes a wave traveling at speed v. So by direct comparison, the wave speed must be:

v² = 1/(ε₀μ₀) → v = 1/√(ε₀μ₀)

It's not a coincidence — it falls out of the math the moment you combine "changing B makes E" with "changing E makes B." The two electromagnetic constants of the vacuum literally set the speed of any disturbance that propagates through it.

4Plug in the real numbers

Now do the calculation that astonished Maxwell in 1865:

ε₀
8.8542 × 10⁻¹² F/m
measured from capacitors
μ₀
1.2566 × 10⁻⁶ N/A²
measured from current-carrying wires
ε₀ × μ₀ = 1.1127 × 10⁻¹⁷ s²/m²
√(ε₀ × μ₀) = 3.336 × 10⁻⁹ s/m
1 / √(ε₀μ₀) = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s

Maxwell did this by hand. He looked at the answer — a number that already matched the experimentally known speed of light to within measurement error — and wrote one of the most quoted lines in physics history:

"We can scarcely avoid the conclusion that light consists in the transverse undulations of the same medium which is the cause of electric and magnetic phenomena."

Light is not a special kind of thing. It's the same E and B fields you get from a battery and a magnet — just the part of them that has detached from any source and is propagating freely through the vacuum at the speed those two constants demand.
