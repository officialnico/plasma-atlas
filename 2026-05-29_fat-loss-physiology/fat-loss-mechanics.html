Field Guide · Training × Body Composition

How Fat Loss
Actually Works

No magic windows, no foods that "undo" a workout. Just the real biochemistry of where the fat goes and how training fits in.

01 — THE MECHANISM

Where does fat physically go?

Fat doesn't "burn off" or turn into heat or muscle. Stored fat (triglycerides) gets broken down and literally exhaled and urinated out. This is the part almost nobody knows.

Step 1 · Mobilize

Lipolysis

When energy demand > intake, hormones (low insulin, high adrenaline) signal fat cells to release stored triglycerides into the blood as free fatty acids + glycerol.

trigger: calorie deficit
Step 2 · Burn

Oxidation

Muscle and other tissue take up those fatty acids and burn them in the mitochondria for energy, combining them with oxygen.

fat + O₂ → energy
Step 3 · Exit

Exhale & Pee

The byproducts are CO₂ and water. ~84% leaves as carbon dioxide through your lungs; the rest exits as water (sweat, urine, breath).

→ breath + water
The punchline: you literally breathe most of your fat out. Lose 10kg of fat and ~8.4kg of it leaves through your lungs as CO₂. Exercise helps mainly by raising how much energy you demand — not by some special fat-melting effect.
02 — THE ONLY LEVER THAT MATTERS

Energy balance, over time

Fat loss is decided by total energy in vs. out across days and weeks — not meal by meal. One shake doesn't "cancel" one run. The whole ledger does.

Energy OUT

BMR (just being alive)~60–70%
Daily movement / NEAT~15–20%
Exercise (your runs)~5–15%
Digesting food (TEF)~10%

Energy IN

Carbs4 cal/g
Protein4 cal/g
Fat9 cal/g
Alcohol7 cal/g
OUT > IN, sustained = fat loss. That's the whole equation.
03 — WHAT DOESN'T WORK THE WAY YOU THINK

Fasted cardio & the "undo" myth

✕ MYTH

"Eating after a fasted run undoes it"

Nothing gets reversed. The run burned what it burned. Food after is just calories on the day's ledger.

✓ REALITY

Refueling with carbs + protein post-run aids recovery and protects muscle. It only "hurts" the cut if it pushes your daily total over maintenance.

✕ MYTH

"Fasted cardio burns way more fat"

You may burn slightly more fat during the run, but the body compensates over the rest of the day.

✓ REALITY

24-hour fat loss ends up roughly equal. Fasted vs. fed is a personal-preference / gut-comfort choice, not a fat-loss cheat code.

✕ MYTH

"Sugar after a workout makes you fat"

Post-exercise, muscles are primed to pull glucose straight into glycogen, not store it as fat.

✓ REALITY

Fast carbs (like fruit) right after training is one of the best-timed times to eat them. Context beats the "sugar = bad" rule.

✕ MYTH

"Cutting harder fixes skinny-fat"

Eating less on low protein strips muscle — making you smaller and softer, not leaner.

✓ REALITY

Skinny-fat is a muscle problem. Fix = enough food, high protein, resistance training. Recomp, not starvation.

04 — WHAT YOUR BODY BURNS, WHEN

The fuel hierarchy

Your body always burns a mix. Intensity shifts the ratio — but total calories burned, not the fat/carb ratio of any single session, drives fat loss.

Rest / easy
~ higher % FAT
Easy run
fat + carb mix
Tempo / 4×4
mostly CARBS (glycogen)
Sprint
almost all CARBS
Don't chase the "fat-burning zone." Easy effort burns a higher percentage of fat but fewer total calories. Hard effort burns more total energy and digs into glycogen, which still creates the deficit that loses fat over the week. Both have a place — that's why your plan mixes them.
05 — THE MACRO YOU DON'T CUT

Protein protects the engine

1.6–2.2g per kg bodyweight / day

In a deficit, your body will break down muscle for energy unless you give it a reason not to. High protein + training is that reason. It also keeps you full and costs the most calories to digest.

At ~82 kg that's roughly 130–160 g/day. Spread across 3–4 meals, ~30–40 g each.

Tying it to your block

Cutting + ramping marathon volume pull opposite directions. Keep the deficit small (~0.25–0.5 kg/week), keep protein high, fuel hard around your Tuesday/Saturday sessions, and take the deficit from easy/rest days. That loses fat while keeping the muscle and the running intact.
