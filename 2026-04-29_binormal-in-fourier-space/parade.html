What you're seeing, top to bottom:
1. The street — marchers (red dots) walking past you (gold figure). Faster v = they zip by; v=0 = stationary; negative v = parading backward.
2. Density curve f(x) — smooth line showing how packed the crowd is at each location.
3. Your readout — the density right at your spot (count of nearby marchers in last frame), updating live.
4. The math — the slope ∂f/∂x AT your position, multiplied by v, equals how fast f is changing at your spot.

Try these experiments

  1. Stand on the front edge of the bump. Move your slider to about 0.6 (just past the peak). Press play with v positive. Watch your readout: density at your spot is rising because the bump is sweeping toward you. The bump's leading edge is steeper here — bigger ∂f/∂x means a bigger rise rate.
  2. Stand on the back edge. Slide to about 0.4 (just before the peak). With v positive, density at your spot is falling — the bump is moving away, and emptier ground is sweeping in.
  3. Stand on the peak. Slide to 0.5. ∂f/∂x = 0 (top of the bump, momentarily flat). Even though v is fast, the bump is locally symmetric — density at your spot is briefly stable. Then it falls.
  4. Set v = 0. Nothing scrolls. Density at your spot doesn't change at all — even with a steep gradient. Why? Because the term is v · ∂f/∂x, and v = 0 zeros it out.
  5. Try the "step" shape. Stand on the rising edge — sharpest gradient possible. With v positive, density rises rapidly through the step.
  6. Reverse v (drag negative). Everything flips — density rises where it was falling.
