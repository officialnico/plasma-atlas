Poloidal cut

D-shape — slice perpendicular to the donut axis. Sliding y rotates this cut around the torus (toroidally).
x = radial outward. y = binormal (mostly out-of-page here, since B is mostly toroidal in this cut). z = along B (mostly out-of-page, with a small poloidal twist).

Toroidal cut (top-down)

Equatorial slice. Concentric rings = where the D-surfaces intersect this plane. The pink dot marks your y-position.
x = radial outward. y = roughly the toroidal-angle direction (along the rings). z = along B.
The experiment: drag the y-slider. With T₀, n₀, or |B| selected, you'll see nothing change in the colors. That's binormal symmetry — the equilibrium has it. With δT selected, the fluctuation pattern drifts as you slide y. That's what GENE actually computes: the y-structure of the perturbations on top of a y-symmetric background.

In the toroidal view, the pink dot tracks your y-position around the ring. It moves as you slide. The colors stay the same as the dot moves (for T, n, |B|), or drift with it (for δT).
