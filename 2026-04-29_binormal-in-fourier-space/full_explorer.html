The ky Fourier basis (16 discrete modes)

ky index: 0
GENE discretizes y into Ny=16 grid points (here). The Fourier transform represents any field in y as a sum of 16 sine waves with ky = 0, 1, 2, ... (in units of the box wavenumber). Each bar above is one mode's amplitude in the current fluctuation pattern. Click prev/next to step through them.
Top: the selected basis function — a single sine wave in y.
Bottom: the actual fluctuation pattern δT(y) (sum of all modes).
The bottom is just adding up all the top sine waves, weighted by the bar heights in the spectrum. δT(y) = Σ aₖ cos(kᵧ y + φₖ)
Why this works: equilibrium fields (T, n, |B|) are constant in y, so they're just the ky=0 mode. Fluctuations have all the other ky modes. Because the background is y-symmetric, GENE evolves each ky mode independently in the linear part of the equation — saving you from having to resolve all 16 simultaneously on a real-space grid. Step through the modes and watch their wavelengths halve as ky doubles.
