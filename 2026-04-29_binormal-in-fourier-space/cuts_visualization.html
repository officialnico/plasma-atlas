The two cuts of a tokamak

The donut has two natural 2D slices. Poloidal = a knife cut through the bagel, perpendicular to its axis (you see the cross-section circles). Toroidal = a horizontal slice, looking down from above (you see two concentric circles). At any point on either cut, we can drop the local x/y/z frame. Pick which equilibrium field to display, then ask: does it change when you move in y?

Poloidal cut

Slice perpendicular to the donut axis — the "donut cross-section." Nested flux surfaces appear as concentric loops.
Vectors at each point: x = radial (outward from center). y = binormal (tangent to surface, perp to B). z = along B (mostly out of this page, into the toroidal direction).

Toroidal cut

Looking down at the donut from above (equatorial plane). Two concentric circles = inner and outer edges of one flux surface.
Vectors at each point: x = radial. y = binormal — moving along the circumference of the surface, this direction. z = along B (mostly tangent to the circles, with a small poloidal component).
What is "binormally symmetric"? Slide your eye in the y direction in either picture. If the field's value doesn't change, it's symmetric in y.
✓ T₀ — yes, constant in y ✓ n₀ — yes, constant in y ✓ |B| — yes, constant in y* ✗ δT (fluctuation) — no

*|B| is constant in y because y is defined as a direction within a flux surface (and roughly perpendicular to B). |B| varies along z (around the torus the long way: stronger inboard, weaker outboard) and across x (different flux surfaces), but not along y.

So your instinct was right: equilibrium quantities (T, n, |B|, p) are all symmetric in y. Fluctuations (δT, δn, δφ — the things GENE solves for) are not — they have rich structure in y, which is exactly what gets decomposed into ky Fourier modes.
