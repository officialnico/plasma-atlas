What you're seeing: The left grid is your coordinate space — a perfect square grid where every cell is identical. The right grid is real physical space — what those same cells actually look like in the tokamak.

Drag the sliders or tap the presets. gxx stretches/compresses radially. gyy stretches/compresses binormally. gxy skews the grid — when it's nonzero, x and y aren't perpendicular anymore.

The key idea: Your code always works on the left grid (simple, rectangular, easy finite differences). The metric tensor tells it how to interpret results as if it were on the right grid (warped, stretched, physical reality). Same code, different physics — just change the numbers in the matrix.

In GENE: these three numbers change at every z-point along the field line. At the outboard midplane, gyy is large (try the "outboard" preset). At the inboard side, it shrinks. The metric is the translator between your easy computation and the hard geometry.
