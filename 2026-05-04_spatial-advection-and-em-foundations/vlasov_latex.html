Full Vlasov–Poisson · 1D
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + v \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + \frac{F}{m} \frac{\partial f}{\partial v} = 0$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + v \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + \frac{F}{m} \frac{\partial f}{\partial v} = 0
The standard form. All three terms summed equal zero.
Solved for ∂f/∂t (the form we've been computing)
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = -v \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} - \frac{F}{m} \frac{\partial f}{\partial v}$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = -v \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} - \frac{F}{m} \frac{\partial f}{\partial v}
Rearranged so the rate of change at the fixed point is on the left.
Full Vlasov–Maxwell · 3D with magnetic field
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla_x f + \frac{q}{m}\left(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}\right) \cdot \nabla_v f = 0$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla_x f + \frac{q}{m}\left(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}\right) \cdot \nabla_v f = 0
3D phase space (x and v are 3D vectors). The Lorentz force replaces the simple F/m. ∇x and ∇v are the gradients in position and velocity space.
The streaming term, isolated
$$v \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$$
v \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
Drift rate (v) times slope of f along x. Gives rate at which f is swept past a fixed point by spatial motion.
The force term, isolated
$$\frac{F}{m} \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial v}$$
\frac{F}{m} \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial v}
Drift rate (F/m, the acceleration) times slope of f along v. Gives rate at which f is swept past by velocity-space motion.
The gradient vector ∇f in phase space
$$\nabla f = \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial x},\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial v} \right)$$
\nabla f = \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial x},\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial v} \right)
The two slopes packaged into a vector. Points "uphill" on the f(x, v) landscape.
A Maxwellian distribution
$$f(v) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi T}} \exp\!\left(-\frac{v^2}{2T}\right)$$
f(v) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi T}} \exp\!\left(-\frac{v^2}{2T}\right)
Bell curve in velocity. T is temperature. Thermal equilibrium distribution.
Vlasov–Poisson coupling (electrostatic)
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + v \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + \frac{qE}{m} \frac{\partial f}{\partial v} = 0$$
$$\frac{\partial E}{\partial x} = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}, \qquad \rho(x,t) = q\!\int f(x,v,t)\, dv$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + v \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + \frac{qE}{m} \frac{\partial f}{\partial v} = 0 \frac{\partial E}{\partial x} = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}, \qquad \rho(x,t) = q\!\int f(x,v,t)\, dv
Vlasov equation coupled to Poisson's equation. The density (integral of f over v) drives the field, the field drives the particles. This is what the simulator solves.
Liouville's theorem · the heart of Vlasov
$$\frac{df}{dt} = 0$$
\frac{df}{dt} = 0
Along any particle's trajectory, f is constant. f doesn't get destroyed or created — it's just transported around phase space. The Vlasov equation is what you get when you write this out using the chain rule.

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = -(1.5)(0.4) - (0.5)(-0.2) = -0.6 + 0.1 = -0.5
