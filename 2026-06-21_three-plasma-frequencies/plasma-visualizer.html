Three frequencies
of a plasma

A plasma rings in three different ways. Two of them come from a magnetic field bending single particles into circles. The third — the plasma frequency — needs no magnet at all. It is the whole electron sea sloshing.

Frequency 01 / 02 — magnetic, single particle

Gyroradius & gyrofrequency

A single electron in a magnetic field feels a force perpendicular to its motion, so it curves into a circle. The radius of that circle and the rate it goes around are the two gyro quantities.

Radius grows with speed and shrinks with field. A faster electron just carves a wider loop. Crank B up and the loop tightens.

ωc = q·B / m

The rate is set only by B and the particle's mass. Change the energy slider: the circle resizes but the time per lap doesn't budge. That is the surprising part — speed cancels out.

Frequency 03 / 03 — electrostatic, collective

Plasma frequency

Forget the magnet. Grab the light electrons and shove them sideways off the heavy ions. You tear a tiny gap of bare charge — and the plasma yanks them back. They overshoot, get pulled back again, and ring.

ωp = √( n·e² / (ε₀·me) )

This is the answer to your question. It isn't an orbit. Displace the electron slab by a distance x and you build a sheet of bare charge — a capacitor. The field it makes is proportional to x, so it acts exactly like a spring: F = −(ne²/ε₀)·x.

A spring made of electric force, with electron mass as the weight on the end.

Spring + mass = oscillation. Denser plasma = stiffer spring = faster ringing (√n). The ions barely move — they're ~1800× heavier — so it's the electrons' show. Notice the field flips direction every time they cross the middle: that's what keeps it oscillating instead of settling.

Why anyone cares

A radio wave slower than ωp can't push through — the electron sea reacts fast enough to cancel it, and the wave bounces.

That single fact is why AM radio skips off the ionosphere and around the curve of the Earth, why metals are shiny (their electron sea has an ωp up in the ultraviolet, so it reflects all visible light), and why measuring a returned signal's cutoff frequency tells you the density of a plasma you can't touch — from a fusion core to the solar wind.
