00 the object

Forget particles. Track a density in phase space.

A plasma has far too many particles to follow individually. So we describe it with one field, the distribution function f(x, v, t): roughly, how many electrons sit near position x with velocity near v at time t. Its home is the two-dimensional phase space of position and velocity.

In the panel above, warm regions hold more electrons than the smooth thermal background f0(v) and cool regions hold fewer. Everything reduces to evolving this field forward in time. Two effects move it; a third closes the loop.

f(x,v,t)=f0(v)+δf,  f0=e−v²/2/2π

01 the two motions

Streaming shears it. The field bends it.

The Vlasov equation says f is conserved as it rides along particle trajectories. Written out, it carries two transport terms plus the field:

∂f/∂t+v ∂f/∂x−E ∂f/∂v=0

Free streaming (v ∂f/∂x): each velocity slice drifts at its own speed, so fast electrons outrun slow ones and any smooth bump gets sheared into an ever-thinner diagonal streak. This is the engine that manufactures fine velocity structure — the whole reason the velocity method will need many modes.

Acceleration (−E ∂f/∂v): the electric field pushes electrons up or down in velocity. Below, turn the field on and watch a column of test particles shear under streaming, then get bent and trapped once the field acts.

mode: free streaming

02 the surprise

Landau damping: a wave dies without collisions.

Close the loop with Poisson's equation — the charge imbalance generates the field that then accelerates the electrons:

∂E/∂x=1−∫f dv

Now give the plasma a small density ripple. It launches a wave. In 1946 Landau predicted, against intuition, that the wave decays even though the system is perfectly reversible and lossless. The energy isn't destroyed — it's handed to electrons moving near the wave's phase velocity v ≈ ω/k, the ones that "surf" it. Below: a traveling wave fading at the Landau rate, with resonant surfers gaining energy.

03 why hermite

Expand velocity in a ladder of moments.

Instead of a grid in velocity, expand the v-dependence in Hermite functions Ψn(v) — a Maxwellian times Hermite polynomials. The coefficients Cn(x,t) are physical moments: C0 is density, C1 is momentum, C2 is energy/pressure, and higher ones encode finer velocity shape.

f(x,v,t)=∑n Cn(x,t) Ψn(v)

The payoff: substitute the expansion into Vlasov and the velocity derivatives collapse into a three-term recurrence. Every moment couples only to its immediate neighbors:

∂Cm/∂t=−m ∂xCm−1 −m+1 ∂xCm+1 −m E Cm−1 −ν m Cm

The teal streaming terms move information up and down the ladder; the violet field term injects the self-consistent force at m=1; the amber term is optional collisions, which in Hermite space simply damp mode m at rate νm. That's the entire physics, now in a form a computer loves.

04 the recipe

One spectral method per axis.

SPACE

Fourier in x

Periodic box, single ripple. Derivatives become multiply-by-ik; Poisson becomes Ê = iĈ0/k. Use np.fft.

∂x → ik
VELOCITY

Hermite in v

Track moments Cn(x), not a velocity grid. The recurrence gives the right-hand side directly — no integrals over v.

f → {Cn}
TIME

RK4 in t

Four RHS samples per step. Mind the CFL: dt < 2.8 / (kmax√2N), since the ladder advects at speeds up to √2N.

4th order

That CFL line is the single most common way this solver blows up. The streaming ladder behaves like advection with a maximum speed that grows with the number of Hermite modes, so adding modes tightens the timestep.

06 the catch

Truncation lies. Recurrence is the tell.

The ladder is infinite; your array is not. Cutting it off at CN = 0 is a closure, and it's a fib: the streaming that should carry structure off to higher moments instead reflects back down. After a while the reflected signal re-coheres and the field energy spuriously revives — the kink you can trigger with the recurrence preset above. More modes push the revival later (you saw the floor drop as you raised N); it never disappears on its own.

The honest fix is a touch of collisions, the −νm Cn term. It preferentially erases the highest, finest moments — exactly the ones the truncation mishandles — so the energy keeps falling cleanly. That's how Hermite-Vlasov codes run for long times: enough modes to resolve the physics, a whisker of collisionality to absorb what spills off the top.

Rule of thumb. If your damping curve bottoms out and turns up, you're seeing numerics, not physics. Add modes or add ν before you trust anything past that point.
