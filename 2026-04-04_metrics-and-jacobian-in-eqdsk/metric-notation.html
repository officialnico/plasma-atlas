The Metric Tensor

subscript notation (covariant) — this is what GENE uses:
$$g_{ij} = \begin{pmatrix} g_{xx} & g_{xy} \\ g_{xy} & g_{yy} \end{pmatrix}$$
each component:
$$g_{xx} = \nabla\psi \cdot \nabla\psi = \left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial R}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial Z}\right)^2$$ $$g_{yy} = \nabla\theta \cdot \nabla\theta = \left(\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial R}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial Z}\right)^2$$ $$g_{xy} = \nabla\psi \cdot \nabla\theta = \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial R}\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial R} + \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial Z}\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial Z}$$

Subscript vs Superscript

these are different objects:
$$g_{xy} \quad \text{(subscript — covariant)}$$ $$g^{xy} \quad \text{(superscript — contravariant)}$$
They're matrix inverses of each other:
$$g^{ij} = \left(g_{ij}\right)^{-1}$$
In papers you'll see both. gxx with subscripts measures distances. gxx with superscripts shows up in gradient operators. GENE documentation mostly uses subscripts.

The Jacobian

from the determinant of the metric tensor:
$$J = \sqrt{\det(g_{ij})} = \sqrt{g_{xx}\, g_{yy} - g_{xy}^2}$$
Same parallelogram, same geometry. The g's give you shape, J gives you area.

Distance Formula

the whole point — converting coordinate distances to real distances:
$$ds^2 = g_{xx}\, d\psi^2 + 2\, g_{xy}\, d\psi\, d\theta + g_{yy}\, d\theta^2$$
This is the metric tensor doing its job. If gxy = 0, this simplifies to a rectangle (Pythagorean theorem with stretching). When gxy ≠ 0, the cross term accounts for the skew — it's a parallelogram.
