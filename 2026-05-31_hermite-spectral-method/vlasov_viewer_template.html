What you just watched

Both runs start at 7 knobs — the two beams are already non-Maxwellian, but still far cheaper than the ~60 grid points a grid spends.

Without collisions, the filaments wind tighter and tighter, and the knob-count slams into 60 — the advantage is gone. The grid would have been just as good. This is the failure everyone feared.

With weak collisions, the fine filaments get smoothed away as fast as they form, so the distribution stays simple-enough and the knob-count hovers around 5–10 the whole time — roughly 10× cheaper than the grid, even in this violent state.

That is the mechanism behind the Frei result. The plasma edge is non-Maxwellian — but it's also collisional, and collisions are what keep the velocity distribution from filamenting to infinity. That's why the knob-trick survives the edge. The open stellarator question is whether a stellarator's twisted geometry drives the filamenting faster than collisions can smooth it — i.e. whether you live in the red column or the green one.
