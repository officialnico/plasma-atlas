A magnetic field is a vector at every point in space. Different sources give different patterns. Here are the five formulas you'll meet first — each one rendered as the function it really is, evaluated on a grid.

01 · Infinite wire (current ⊙ out of page)
x = 0.00
y = 0.00
|B| = —

Infinite wire

Bx = −k·y / (x² + y²)
By = +k·x / (x² + y²) k = μ₀I / (2π)

Current flowing straight up out of the page at the origin. The field circles the wire counterclockwise (right-hand rule: thumb up, fingers curl). Magnitude falls off as 1/r — strong near the wire, weak far away.

02 · Magnetic dipole (moment along x-axis)
x = 0.00
y = 0.00
|B| = —

Pure dipole

Bx = k·(x² − y²) / (x² + y²)²
By = k·2xy / (x² + y²)² k = μ₀m / 2π

The idealized "point dipole" — a magnet shrunk to a single point with moment m pointing along +x. Two characteristic lobes: field bursts out the +x side, loops around, returns through the −x side. Falls off as 1/r².

03 · Two point poles (the visualizer model)

Two point poles

Bx = k·[(x−x₊)/r₊² − (x−x₋)/r₋²]
By = k·[(y−y₊)/r₊² − (y−y₋)/r₋²] r± = distance to each pole

A +pole (red, north) and −pole (blue, south) treated as Coulomb-like sources. Same shape as the dipole when poles are close, but you can pull them apart and watch the field stretch. This is exactly the model in your other visualizer.

04 · Uniform field (solenoid interior)

|B| = —

Uniform field

Bx = B₀·cos(θ)
By = B₀·sin(θ) constant everywhere

Same vector at every point. This is what you get inside an ideal solenoid (B = μ₀nI), or far from any localized source. Boring to look at — but it's the cleanest place to do physics, which is why labs use solenoids and Helmholtz coils.

|B| = —

Two parallel wires

B = Bwire₁ + Bwire₂ superposition of two wire fields

Cross-section of two long parallel wires. Same direction currents (both ⊙) attract — fields add between them at the top/bottom and cancel along the line connecting them. Opposite currents (⊙ and ⊗) repel — fields cancel between, add outside.
