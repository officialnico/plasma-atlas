What to look for. Both start identical and roll up into the same cat's-eye vortex — the physics agrees. But the left panel is speckled, and that speckle is the cost of sampling. Now slide the particle count down to 500: the left vortex dissolves into static while the right stays razor-sharp. Slide it back up to 12,000 and the speckle calms — but notice it never fully disappears, and the panel runs slower. That's the √N tax in your hands: to halve the noise you must quadruple the particles.
And the flip side. The right panel pays a tax too — just not where you can see it yet. As the vortex tightens, the heat-map keeps growing finer and finer stripes (the filamentation from your notebook). Eventually those stripes get thinner than the grid can hold. PIC never has that problem; a particle is happy at any velocity. Neither method is free — they just send the bill to different places.
