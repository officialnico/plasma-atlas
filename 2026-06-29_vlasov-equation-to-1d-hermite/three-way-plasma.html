Watch them agree, then differ. All three roll up into the same cat's-eye vortex — the physics is one physics. But look closely: PIC is speckled (slide particles down to 500 and it dissolves into static); grid is smooth but its fine swirls blur where the mesh runs out; Hermite is smooth too, and its coefficient strip tells the real story.
Reading the Hermite recipe strip. Each bar is how much one building-block hump is being used. Early on, only the low bars are lit — a few humps describe two calm beams. As the vortex tightens and grows fine velocity stripes, energy marches up into the high bars. When it reaches the last bar, the recipe has run out of room — that's the truncation, the spectral method's version of "the mesh ran out." A gentle damping on the top modes (a stand-in for collisions) keeps it from exploding.
