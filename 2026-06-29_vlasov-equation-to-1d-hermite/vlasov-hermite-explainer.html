00 · The object on the table

First, what is the thing
we're trying to track?

A plasma is a sea of charged particles. We don't follow each particle — there are far too many. Instead we track one object: the distribution function f(x, v, t).

Read it like a heat-map of "how much stuff is here, moving this fast." It lives in phase space — position on one axis, velocity on the other. A tall ridge at v = 0 means most particles are sitting still; a hump out at high v means a fast-moving beam.

The Vlasov equation just says how this heat-map sloshes around over time as particles stream and feel electric forces:

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\;+\;\underbrace{v\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}}_{\text{streaming}}\;+\;\underbrace{\frac{q}{m}E\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}}_{\text{pushed by the field}}\;=\;0$$

That's the whole game: evolve f in time. The hard part is that f is a function over a 2-D space, and the velocity direction loves to grow absurdly fine structure. The hero image above is exactly that happening — watch the smooth blob get combed into thinner and thinner stripes. Hold that thought; it comes back to bite us.

The cast, in plain words

f(x,v,t) — the distribution. The star.

x — where a particle is.

v — how fast it's going (and which way).

E(x,t) — the electric field the particles make and feel.

q, m — charge and mass of the species.

"1-D" here means one position axis and one velocity axis — the smallest setting where everything is real but nothing is buried in extra indices. Physicists call it 1D1V.

01 · Three ways to chase a distribution

Everyone agrees on the goal.
Nobody agrees on how.

There are three big families of methods. They differ in one question: how do you write down a smooth function on a computer that only holds numbers?

Particle-in-Cell · PIC

Throw dice

Represent f with millions of fake "super-particles" and just push them around. Intuitive, flexible, dominant in practice.

+Simple, handles wild geometry
—Statistical noise; needs huge particle counts to get quiet
Eulerian · grid

Tile the floor

Lay a fine mesh over the whole (x,v) plane and store f in every cell. Noise-free and direct.

+No noise, very accurate
—Cost explodes; the velocity mesh must be insanely fine
Spectral · Hermite-Fourier

Sum smooth waves

Don't store values at all. Store the weights of a few smooth building-block functions whose sum is the distribution. This is our path.

+No noise; a Maxwellian costs one number
—Sharp velocity structure needs many terms

The spectral bet: most of the time a plasma's velocity distribution is close to a smooth bell curve. If your building blocks already look like bell curves, you need very few of them. That single idea is why the Hermite basis exists.

02 · The building blocks

Meet the Hermite functions
(and why n = 0 is special)

These are our smooth "waves of velocity." Each one, Ψₙ(ξ), is a Hermite polynomial wrapped in a Gaussian. Toggle them on and off — and notice the very first one.

Ψ₀ is literally a Maxwellian

Turn off everything except n = 0. That curve is a Gaussian — the exact shape of a plasma sitting in thermal equilibrium. So the lowest building block is already the answer for a calm plasma. That's the magic: equilibrium costs a single number.

The higher ones, n = 1, 2, 3…, are increasingly wiggly. They're the corrections you add when the distribution leans, spreads, sprouts a beam, or develops fine ripples. More wiggle = finer velocity detail.

$$\Psi_n(\xi)=\left(\pi\,2^{n} n!\right)^{-1/2} H_n(\xi)\,e^{-\xi^{2}}$$

Hₙ is the n-th Hermite polynomial, e^(−ξ²) is the Gaussian envelope, and the ugly prefactor is just bookkeeping so the functions are "orthonormal" — a property we'll cash in next. The variable ξ = (v − u)/α is just velocity, re-centred on the mean flow u and rescaled by the thermal speed α so the basis sits where the action is.

Read the bars like a story. A plain Maxwellian: only C₀ lights up — one number. A flowing beam needs C₁ too. A two-stream (two humps) or a bump-on-tail needs a long tail of coefficients, because those shapes carry real kinetic detail. The number of modes you need is a direct measure of how "non-thermal" your plasma is.

Mathematically, "use N_H modes" means this finite sum — and finding the weights is just a projection, thanks to orthonormality:

$$\hat f(x,v,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{N_H-1} C_n(x,t)\,\Psi_n(\xi)\qquad\quad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Psi_n\,\Psi^{m}\,d\xi=\delta_{n,m}$$

The δ ("Kronecker delta") is 1 when n=m and 0 otherwise. It means the blocks don't overlap when measured the right way — so each weight Cₙ can be read off cleanly without untangling it from the others. That clean separability is the engine of everything below.

04 · Turning the equation inside out

From one PDE in (x, v)
to a stack of PDEs in x

Now the actual conversion. We stop solving for f and start solving for its coefficients Cₙ(x,t). The mechanical recipe is always the same three moves — and each term of the Vlasov equation transforms in its own way. Click a term to see what it becomes.

$\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial t}$ + $v\,\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ + $\dfrac{q}{m}E\,\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial v}$ = 0

Substitute the recipe

Replace f with its Hermite sum Σ Cₙ Ψₙ inside the Vlasov equation. Nothing is approximated yet — we've just changed how f is written.

Multiply by Ψₘ and integrate over velocity

This is the "Galerkin projection." Because the blocks are orthonormal, integrating Ψₙ·Ψₘ wipes out every term except n = m — so a tangled sum collapses into one clean equation per mode.

Apply the two ladder identities

Multiplying a Hermite block by v turns it into its neighbours (m±1) — the recursion relation. Differentiating it in v nudges it to m−1 — the derivative relation. These two facts are what couple the modes into a ladder.

$$\hat v\,\Psi_n=\alpha\!\sqrt{\tfrac{n+1}{2}}\,\Psi_{n+1}+\alpha\!\sqrt{\tfrac{n}{2}}\,\Psi_{n-1}+u\,\Psi_n,\qquad \frac{d\Psi_n}{d\hat v}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\sqrt{2(n+1)}\;\Psi_{n+1}$$

Read off the result

What's left is one ordinary-in-velocity, partial-in-space equation for each coefficient Cₘ. This single line is the 1-D Hermite form of the Vlasov equation:

$$\frac{\partial C_m}{\partial t}+\alpha\!\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{m+1}{2}}\frac{\partial C_{m+1}}{\partial x}+\sqrt{\tfrac{m}{2}}\frac{\partial C_{m-1}}{\partial x}+\frac{u}{\alpha}\frac{\partial C_m}{\partial x}\right)-\frac{q}{m}\frac{\sqrt{2m}}{\alpha}\,E\,C_{m-1}=0$$

And Gauss's law, run through the same machine, only ever touches the lowest coefficient — because density is C₀:

$$\frac{\partial E}{\partial x}=\sum_s q_s\,\alpha_s\,C_0^{\,s}(x)$$

Step back and look at the shape of it. Mode m only ever talks to m−1 and m+1. The infinite, 2-D problem became a tidy tridiagonal ladder of 1-D equations. That structure is the entire payoff.

05 · Why the coefficients aren't abstract

The bottom rungs of the ladder
are just fluid physics

Here's the part that makes the whole thing feel less like a math trick. The low coefficients aren't arbitrary — they're the quantities you already know from fluid dynamics. C₀ is density. C₁ is momentum. C₂ is pressure. And so on up the ladder.

Plug those identities into the bottom equation of the ladder and the continuity equation falls out. Plug them into the next rung and you get conservation of momentum. Truncating the Hermite series at N_H modes is exactly the same as keeping N_H fluid moments — it's the missing "closure" that fluid modellers always have to guess at, handed to you for free.

So the dial in section 03 wasn't just numerical resolution. It's a slider between fluid (few modes, smooth, cheap) and fully kinetic (many modes, every velocity wiggle resolved). Same equations, one knob.

$$C_0=n\;\Rightarrow\;\frac{\partial n}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left(v_{\text{avg}}\,n\right)=0\quad\text{(continuity, for free)}$$

06 · The honest catch

Remember the combing
in the hero image?

As a plasma evolves, free-streaming shears the distribution into ever-finer velocity stripes — filamentation. Fine stripes mean high Hermite modes get excited, and a truncated ladder can't hold them. Energy that should keep flowing up the ladder hits the truncation wall and bounces back down.

Recurrence

The bounced-back signal makes the simulation falsely "recur" — the plasma appears to return to its initial state at a fixed time set by how many modes you kept. More modes pushes the false echo later, but never removes it. The recurrence time grows only like √N_H — diminishing returns.

The fix: a little collisionality

Add a gentle collision operator that preferentially damps the highest modes — the ones holding the unphysically-fine stripes. Physically it's the smoothing that real collisions provide; numerically it's a soft wall that absorbs the runaway instead of reflecting it.

$$\mathcal{C}[C_m]=\nu\,\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)}{(N_H-1)(N_H-2)(N_H-3)}\,C_m$$

Why the cubic weight? It's nearly zero for low m (so it barely touches the physics you trust — density, momentum, energy) and ramps up hard near the truncation N_H (so it crushes only the junk piling against the wall). Conservation of the things that matter, quiet disposal of the things that don't.

Where to go next

You now have the whole arc

Track f → it's hard because velocity grows fine structure → write f as a sum of smooth Hermite blocks → project the Vlasov equation onto them → get a tidy ladder of coefficient equations whose bottom rungs are fluid physics → tame the filamentation with a touch of collisionality. That's the 1-D Hermite spectral method, end to end.
