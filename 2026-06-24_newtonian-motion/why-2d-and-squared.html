Why 2d,
and why squared

Stopping distance is the area of a triangle. That one fact answers both questions: a triangle is half its rectangle (the 2), and doubling the speed stretches both of its sides at once (the square).

F · d = ½ m v2  ⟶  F = m v2 ⁄ 2d

The 2 — it's a triangle

The block never travels at full speed the whole way; it ramps from v down to 0, averaging v⁄2. Distance = average speed × time, so the filled triangle is exactly half the rectangle. That ½ is the 2 in 2d.

The square — two sides grow

Go twice as fast and two things double together: you cover ground twice as fast (height) and it takes twice as long to stop (base). Two factors of speed multiply, so the area — the stopping distance — grows as speed².

Halve the stopping distance and you double the force. That is the whole reason for crumple zones and airbags.
They cannot reduce the momentum you must shed — only stretch d, dropping the force.
