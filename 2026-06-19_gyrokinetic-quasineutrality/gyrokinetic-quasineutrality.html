ACT 00THE ORDERING — WHAT MAKES THIS LEGAL

One small number, and one that isn't.

Gyrokinetics is an organized expansion in a single small parameter ε. Everything slow, weak, and stretched-along-the-field is order ε:

the gyrokinetic ordering

The trick is the last relation. While the gyroradius ρ is tiny next to the machine size L, the turbulence has perpendicular wavelengths comparable to the gyroradius itself:

the one quantity that stays O(1)

That is the whole reason finite-Larmor-radius (FLR) physics survives the averaging. A particle doesn't sample the potential at a point — it samples it around a ring whose size is the same order as the wavelength it's smearing over. Keep that ring in mind. It's the protagonist.

equilibrium scale L (slow) gyro-ring ρ ~ wavelength

Big cool envelope: the slowly varying equilibrium. The hot ring is small against it — but the wave packet it sits in has ripples its own size. ρ/L ≪ 1, yet k⊥ρ ~ 1.

ACT 01THE RING — A PARTICLE IS NOT ITS GYROCENTER

The charge lives on a ring. The bookkeeping lives at the center.

+0.00φ felt right now
+0.00φ at gyrocenter

Background shading is the potential φ(x)=cos(k·x). The hot dot is the real particle; the cool dot is its gyrocenter R. The particle's true position is x = R + ρ(θ) — offset by the gyroradius vector, spinning at the gyrofrequency Ω.

Split the position into the slow part you track and the fast part you want gone:

particle position = gyrocenter + ring

As the particle whips around at Ω = qB/m, the value of φ it actually feels swings up and down — watch the hot readout. The gyrocenter sits at one fixed point in the wave; the particle tours a whole ring of values around it.

So the physically meaningful quantity isn't φ at a point. It's φ averaged around the ring, holding the gyrocenter fixed. That average is the entire game — and remarkably, it has a closed form. That's Act 02.

The move: we will never solve for the particle. We solve for the gyrocenter, then ask what the field equation needs back in particle space. Every subtlety lives in that round trip.
ACT 02THE GYROAVERAGE — WHERE J₀ COMES FROM

Average a plane wave over a circle. You get a Bessel function.

Take a Fourier mode φ ∝ e^{ik·x}. Put the particle at x = R + ρ and average over the gyrophase θ at fixed gyrocenter R. The e^{ik·R} factor is constant under the average and pulls out; what's left is the average of e^{ik·ρ}:

the gyroaverage of a plane wave

With |ρ| = v⊥/Ω and k·ρ = k⊥ρ cosθ, that integral is literally the textbook integral representation of J₀. No approximation — it's exact. Drag the slider and watch the ring's average trace out the Bessel curve.

Read it off: at k⊥ρ → 0 the ring is tiny, the wave looks flat, J₀ → 1 — no averaging effect. As k⊥ρ grows, the ring spans peaks and troughs that cancel, so J₀ falls and rings. That decay is FLR screening, particle by particle.

ACT 03POLARIZATION — THE PRICE OF THE ROUND TRIP

Going back to particle space leaves a charge behind.

Turn on a potential gradient. Each gyro-orbit's center is displaced by the E×B response — and because that displacement depends on φ, the back-transform from gyrocenters to particles leaves a net polarization charge.

The field equation needs charge density at particle positions. But we evolved gyrocenters. Transforming back isn't free: the gyrocenter coordinate itself was shifted by an amount proportional to φ (that's the generating function of the gyrocenter transform). So the particle density splits into two pieces:

particle density = gyroaveraged gyrocenter density + polarization

The first term is the honest gyrocenter charge, smeared back out by the same J₀ from Act 02. The second term — the polarization density n_pol — is the part proportional to φ. It's a response, not a source. That's why, in the field equation, it belongs on the left-hand side as a screening operator.

Physical picture: a uniform sea of gyrocenters, once you account for finite rings shifting in a field, is not charge-neutral at the particle level. The plasma polarizes — exactly like a dielectric. That dielectric response is what replaces the vacuum ε₀∇².
ACT 04SUMMING THE RINGS — WHERE Γ₀ COMES FROM

Real particles have a spread of speeds. Average J₀² over a Maxwellian.

Each particle screens by its own J₀(k⊥ρ) on the way in (averaging the potential it feels) and again by J₀ on the way out (smearing its charge back). That's a factor of J₀². But ρ = v⊥/Ω varies with speed — so integrate over the Maxwellian:

the velocity-space average of J₀²

The integral collapses to a single named function: Γ₀(b) = I₀(b)e^{−b}, with b = (k⊥ρ_i)² set by the thermal gyroradius. The shaded rings on the right are weighted by the Maxwellian; their summed J₀² is computed live and lands exactly on the Γ₀ curve.

This is the screening function. Γ₀ → 1 at long wavelength (no screening), Γ₀ → 0 at short wavelength (the plasma fully shields it). Everything in the field solve is built from 1 − Γ₀.

ACT 05ASSEMBLE — AND MEET THE OLD POISSON LIMIT

Quasineutrality, with the polarization moved to the left.

Set total particle charge density to zero across species. The φ-proportional polarization from Act 03, now carrying the (1−Γ₀) from Act 04, moves to the left as a screening operator. The honest gyrocenter charge stays on the right as the source:

gyrokinetic quasineutrality — the field equation the codes actually solve

What you learned in E&M

A Laplacian acting on φ, sourced by the literal charge density. The plasma's own response is nowhere in it.

What gyrokinetics solves

No ε₀∇² in sight — and that's correct. With λ_D ≪ ρ_i the vacuum term is smaller than the polarization term by (λ_D/ρ_i)². The plasma's dielectric response is the operator.

One more thing, to connect it to the intuition you arrived with. Expand 1−Γ₀ for small b:

the long-wavelength limit recovers a familiar operator

At small b, the screening operator is k⊥²ρ_i² — which back in real space is precisely the −ρ_i²∇⊥² "modified Poisson" term people quote. So your instinct ("it looks like a screened Poisson equation") was the b ≪ 1 corner of the exact thing. The full Γ₀ is what you need the moment k⊥ρ_i stops being small — which, per Act 00, is exactly the regime turbulence lives in.

exact: 1 − Γ₀(b) long-λ: b = k⊥²ρ²

They kiss at the origin and diverge fast. The straight line (the differential operator) over-screens at high k; the exact Γ₀ saturates. Drag Act 04's slider — the marker tracks here too.

What this becomes in the code

In a flux-tube code (GENE, GX, stella) the perpendicular plane is spectral, so k⊥ is a grid label, not a derivative. That turns J₀(k⊥ρ) and Γ₀(b) into per-mode scalar multipliers — you precompute them on the velocity grid and the "Poisson solve" becomes an algebraic division by Σ (q²n₀/T)(1−Γ₀) at each k⊥. The hard part isn't the solve; it's getting the velocity integral, the species sum, and the boundary/zonal-flow piece right.
