what you're looking at

Orbits are just straight lines in a curved space.

In flat space, a thing with no force on it travels in a straight line forever. Einstein's idea is that this never stops being true — mass doesn't pull on the marble with a force, it changes the shape of the space the marble is moving through. The marble keeps going as straight as it can; the straightest possible path through a curved region just happens to curve. That's an orbit. That's a falling apple. No tug-of-war, no rope — just geometry.

Turn on binary to watch two masses carve moving wells around each other, and the whole sheet flexes to keep up. Crank the mass slider and the funnel deepens — more mass, more curvature, steeper "downhill" for everything nearby.

One honest caveat — because you're clearly trying to really get this

This rubber-sheet picture is a teaching crutch, and it quietly cheats in two ways. First, it shows a 2D sheet bending into a 3rd dimension, but real spacetime is 4D curving with no "outside" to bend into — there's no literal down. Second, and this is the big one: for slow everyday objects, the gravity you feel is mostly the curvature of time, not space. The apple falls because clocks run slightly slower near the ground, and the apple's path through spacetime bends toward where its own time ticks slowest. The sheet shows you space curving, which is the easy part to draw — but the part that actually drops the apple is the time-curvature you saw two steps ago. Same machinery, just hard to render as a mesh.

Your question: is there a "zero gravity" to measure from?

Yes — and this is where gravity differs from motion. With speed there's no "really at rest," no preferred frame. But gravitational time dilation does have a natural zero: a clock infinitely far from all mass, where the pull drops to nothing, ticks at the maximum rate. Every other clock runs slow relative to that. The clock hands in the visual sweep at their local rate, and the readout compares the slowest one to that ∞ reference set at 1.00×. The honest catch: it's a limit you approach, never a spot you reach — in a universe with mass everywhere, nowhere is truly at zero. So it's a fastest-possible baseline, not a master clock sitting at the middle of the universe.

So tying it all together: there's no center, no master clock — just clocks compared to clocks. Motion stretches time by γ. Mass curves spacetime, and that curvature is what we call gravity. The marbles aren't being pulled. They're coasting in straight lines across a floor that mass tilted underneath them.
