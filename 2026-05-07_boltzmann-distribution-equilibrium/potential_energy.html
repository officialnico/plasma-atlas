Forget formulas for a second. Potential energy is what the universe owes you for putting something somewhere it doesn't want to be — lifted up against gravity, pushed against a spring, pulled away from a magnet. That debt gets paid back as motion the moment you let go.

The four scenes below let you build the intuition from the ground up. Drag things, release them, watch the bookkeeping. By the end you'll see why "potential energy" is just a clever way of talking about where motion comes from — and why the Boltzmann formula puts it in the exponent.

— I

Lift the ball. Drop it. Watch the trade.

Drag the ball up to any height, then release. As it falls, height converts into speed. The two bars on the right are bookkeeping — potential energy (red) drains, kinetic energy (blue) fills. Their sum (green) stays constant. This is what conservation of energy means physically.

— what to notice The ball doesn't "have" energy by sitting up there in some mysterious way. It has potential energy because it took work to put it there — work you did against gravity. — II

Energy doesn't disappear. It just changes form.

A ball in a bowl. Push it up the side, release, and it rolls back and forth forever (in a frictionless world). At the bottom: all kinetic, zero potential. At the edges: all potential, zero kinetic. The two trade places, perfectly, twice every cycle.

— what to notice The graph at the top tracks PE and KE over time — they're mirror images. Add a tiny bit of friction and the total slowly bleeds away. That's where energy actually "goes": friction converts it to heat (random molecular motion), which is technically still kinetic energy — just spread across so many particles you can't track it. This is exactly the bridge to Boltzmann.
— III

Every potential energy is just a landscape.

Here's the big idea. Forget gravity, springs, charges. Just imagine a hilly surface. Wherever a ball is on the surface — that height is its potential energy. Balls roll downhill. They speed up going down (PE → KE) and slow down going up (KE → PE). That's the entire concept. Every force in physics has a landscape like this.

— what to notice Try the double well. A ball in one well can't reach the other unless you give it enough KE to climb the barrier between them. Add friction → balls eventually settle at the bottom of whichever well they started in. This is exactly why molecules sit in stable configurations, why electrons orbit at specific energy levels, why a ball at rest stays at rest. "Stable" just means "at the bottom of a valley in the energy landscape."
— IV

Charges have a landscape too. That's the plasma connection.

A positive charge sitting in space creates a potential energy landscape for any other charge. For another positive charge, it's a hill centered on the source — like charges repel, so getting close costs energy. For a negative charge (electron), it's a well — opposite charges attract, getting close releases energy. Drag the test charge anywhere and watch its potential energy change.

— closing the loop Now reread your Boltzmann formula: n = n₀ · exp(−U/kBT). U is the potential energy of a particle at that location — the height of the landscape right there. kBT is the thermal energy — how much "climbing power" each particle has on average. The formula is just asking, "how many thermal-energy-units tall is this hill?" Tiny hills (U ≪ kBT) → particles barely notice → density nearly uniform. Huge hills (U ≫ kBT) → almost no particles make it up → density crashes exponentially. That's the whole story.
