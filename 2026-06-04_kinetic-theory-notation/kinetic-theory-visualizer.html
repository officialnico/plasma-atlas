and the moments are really saying

Two live worlds. On the left, phase space: every particle is a dot at its position x and velocity v. Drag a box around them and watch f·dx·dv turn into an actual headcount. Below, the same particles' velocities become a curve, and you can integrate it live to see each moment appear.

01 — the phase-space box

f(x,v,t)·dx·dv = a number of particles

Position runs left–right, velocity runs bottom–top, so each dot says where a particle is and how fast it's going. f is a density — how crowded the dots are at a spot — not a count by itself. Multiply that crowding by the size of a tiny box dx·dv and the units cancel to leave a plain number: how many particles live in that box.

02 — the moments

Boil f down: count, drift, spread

Collapse the cloud onto the velocity axis and you get the velocity distribution — how many particles travel at each speed. Every measurable quantity is this curve summed over all velocities (that's ∫…dv), first multiplied by a power of v that chooses what you measure. Pick a moment below and hit integrate to watch the sum build up.

The one idea underneath all three views: a density only becomes a number when you multiply it by a chunk of the space it lives in — a box dx dv, or a sliver dv swept across the whole axis by the integral. Change the weight (1, v, or (v−u)²) and the very same sum hands you how many, where the centre is, or how wide.

m = 1, domain length L = 10. f(x,v) = (N/L)·Gaussian(v; u, σ) with σ = √T.
