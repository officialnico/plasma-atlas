[01] Radial pressure profile · the pedestal

Plasma pressure from the hot core (left) to the cool edge (right). In H-mode an edge transport barrier suppresses turbulence and lets a steep pedestal stand up — lifting the whole profile.

L-mode profile H-mode profile transport barrier edge turbulence (eddies)

What to notice: below threshold the edge pressure droops to ~zero and eddies churn freely. Cross the threshold and the eddies near the edge calm into sheared layers — turbulence is sheared apart — producing the steep pedestal cliff.

[03] ELM cycle · pedestal build-up & crash ⚠ DIVERTOR HEAT LIMIT

Live trace. The pedestal pressure ramps up until it hits the peeling–ballooning stability limit — then the edge crashes, dumping a burst of heat onto the divertor. Higher power → faster, bigger Type-I ELMs.

pedestal pressure stability limit / ELM crash divertor heat flux

The trade-off: ELMs flush out impurities and helium ash (good), but each Type-I burst can erode or melt divertor surfaces (bad). On a reactor like ITER they must be mitigated — pacing them smaller and more frequent, or suppressing them with magnetic perturbations.
