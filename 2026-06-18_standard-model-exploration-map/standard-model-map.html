Lesson 01 — The big split

There are only two kinds of thing

Before any names, get the shape of it. The particle zoo divides cleanly in two. Some particles are the stuff; others are the messengers that let stuff push and pull on other stuff. Hold this distinction and the rest falls into place.

Fermions — the matter

Stuff that takes up space

Quarks and leptons. They obey a rule (the Pauli exclusion principle) that forbids two of them from sharing the same state — which is exactly why matter is solid and you don't fall through your chair. Spin ½.

Bosons — the forces

Messengers that carry pushes & pulls

When two particles interact, they swap a force-carrying boson. Photons carry electromagnetism, gluons the strong force, W and Z the weak force. Happy to pile up in the same place — that's why light beams pass through each other. Spin 1.

And then there's the Higgs — a one-off (spin 0) that explains where mass comes from. We'll get to it.

Lesson 03 — Why three columns?

Nature shipped three copies

Here's a genuine mystery the Standard Model describes but doesn't explain: matter comes in three near-identical generations. Each has the same pattern of charges — only the masses climb steeply. Everything around you is made from Generation I; the heavier two appear in cosmic rays and colliders, then decay almost instantly.

Generation I

Everyday matter

up, down, electron, neutrino. Builds every atom in your body and the planet under you.

Heavier echoes

The heavyweights

Lesson 06 — The famous one

The Higgs: where mass comes from

Empty space isn't empty — it's filled with the Higgs field. As particles move through it, they get slowed down, and that resistance is what we measure as mass. Particles that ignore the field (the photon) stay massless and move at light speed; particles that interact strongly (the top quark) feel heavy.

In 2012, the Large Hadron Collider gave the field a hard enough "kick" to make a ripple in it pop out as a real particle — the Higgs boson, around 125 GeV. Finding it confirmed the last missing piece of the model, nearly 50 years after it was predicted.

Mental model

Picture a celebrity crossing a crowded room. The crowd (the Higgs field) clusters around them, making them hard to get moving and hard to stop — that's mass. A nobody walks straight through untouched — that's a massless particle.

Lesson 07 — The edge of the map

What the model can't explain

The Standard Model is the most precisely tested theory in science — and physicists are certain it's incomplete. Here's what falls off the edge. Knowing these is what takes you from hero to wanting more.

Gravity is missing

The fourth force has no place in the model. No one has found the "graviton," and gravity and quantum mechanics refuse to be written in the same language.

Dark matter & dark energy

About 95% of the universe's content is invisible to the Standard Model. None of the 17 particles fit the bill for the dark matter that holds galaxies together.

Where did antimatter go?

The Big Bang should have made equal matter and antimatter, which would annihilate to nothing. Yet here we are. The model can't fully account for the imbalance.

Neutrinos shouldn't have mass

The original model said neutrinos were massless — but they oscillate between flavors, which requires mass. A confirmed crack in the theory.
