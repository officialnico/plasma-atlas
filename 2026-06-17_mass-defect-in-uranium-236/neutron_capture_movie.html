The honest details (the things I refused to simplify away)
  1. The real strong-force well is tens of MeV deep, but the lower neutron orbitals in ²³⁶U are already filled (Pauli exclusion). So the net energy released when the last neutron is added — the neutron separation energy Sn(²³⁶U) ≈ 6.5 MeV — is the number animated here, not the full well depth.
  2. De-excitation is genuinely a cascade: the excited ²³⁶U* drops through several discrete nuclear energy levels, emitting multiple gamma lines whose energies sum to ≈ Sn. The five photons shown stand in for that real spectrum.
  3. Momentum is conserved at every emission: each photon recoils the nucleus slightly (you can see the cluster nudge). The recoil kinetic energy is minuscule and is left out of the energy budget for clarity.
  4. A thermal neutron on ²³⁵U more often causes fission; radiative capture (the n,γ branch shown) is the channel that actually produces ²³⁶U — which is the nucleus your mass question is about.
  5. "Mass" here means rest-frame total energy ÷ c². Binding energy is negative and counts inside that total, so a bound system weighs less than its separated parts: m(²³⁶U)=236.0456 u vs m(²³⁵U)+m(n)=236.0526 u; the gap 0.0070 u × 931.494 = 6.5 MeV is exactly the escaped gamma energy.
