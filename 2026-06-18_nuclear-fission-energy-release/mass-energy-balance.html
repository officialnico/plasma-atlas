Mass–Energy Equivalence

A bond has weight.

Pull two things together and energy leaves the system; force them together against their will and energy is stored inside it. Either way the books are settled in mass, through E = mc². Drag the magnets — or switch to a nucleus — and watch the balance decide whether the whole weighs more or less than its separated parts.

The reference is always the two bodies infinitely far apart, where the interaction energy is zero. Attracting bodies release energy as they approach, so the close, bound configuration carries less energy and therefore less mass than the separated parts. Repelling bodies must have energy pushed in to bring them close, so they weigh more. The needle direction is exact; its size tracks how much interaction energy is in play, not the true magnitude — that lives in the numbers. For the magnets the effect is around 10⁻¹⁷ kg, hopelessly unmeasurable. For the nucleons it is a fraction of a percent — real, and weighed every day in mass spectrometers. Same equation; fourteen orders of magnitude apart.
