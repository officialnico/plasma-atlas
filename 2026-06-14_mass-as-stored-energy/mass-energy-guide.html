01 From ½mv² to γmc²

Einstein already contains Newton

The cleanest way to trust E = γmc² is to watch the kinetic energy you learned in first year fall out of it as the leading correction. That is a Taylor-series statement, nothing more.

Special relativity hands us the Lorentz factor, where β = v/c:

$$\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}}=(1-\beta^2)^{-1/2}$$

Now apply the binomial series — the Taylor expansion of $(1+x)^n$ about $x=0$ — with $x=-\beta^2$ and $n=-\tfrac12$. Since particles we meet have $\beta\ll1$, $x$ is small and the series converges fast:

$$(1+x)^{n}=1+nx+\frac{n(n-1)}{2!}x^2+\cdots$$
$$\gamma=1+\tfrac{1}{2}\beta^{2}+\tfrac{3}{8}\beta^{4}+\tfrac{5}{16}\beta^{6}+\cdots$$

Multiply by the rest energy $mc^2$. The total energy splits into a piece that never goes away and a piece that depends on motion:

$$E=\gamma mc^{2}=\underbrace{mc^{2}}_{\text{rest energy}}+\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}mv^{2}}_{\text{Newtonian KE}}+\underbrace{\tfrac{3}{8}m\frac{v^4}{c^2}+\cdots}_{\text{relativistic tail}}$$
The second term is exactly the kinetic energy from first-year mechanics. It was never a separate law — it is the first non-trivial term of a Taylor expansion. The first term, the rest energy, is the seed of the whole question.

02 Mass is a length

The invariant norm of the four-momentum

Energy is frame-dependent; run alongside a particle and its energy drops. So energy alone cannot be mass. Mass is the one combination of energy and momentum that every observer agrees on — and geometrically it is a length.

Stack time and space into one object, the four-position, and its rate of change (per unit proper time) scaled by mass is the four-momentum:

$$x^{\mu}=(ct,\;x,\;y,\;z),\qquad p^{\mu}=\left(\tfrac{E}{c},\;p_x,\;p_y,\;p_z\right)$$

The catch from year-two linear algebra: this space doesn't use the ordinary dot product. The "metric" that measures length flips the sign of the space part (particle-physics convention $+\,-\,-\,-$):

$$\eta_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$$

Contract the four-momentum with itself through that metric. Mixed terms vanish; the result is a single number, identical in every inertial frame:

$$p^{\mu}p_{\mu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}=\left(\tfrac{E}{c}\right)^{2}-|\vec{p}\,|^{2}=(mc)^{2}$$

Rearranging gives the relation in the hero — and the punchline:

$$\boxed{\;E^{2}=(pc)^{2}+(mc^{2})^{2}\;}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad m=\frac{E_{0}}{c^{2}}$$
Set p = 0 (the rest frame) and only the mass term survives: mass is the rest-frame energy. The "stored energy" slogan is precisely this — provided you say rest-frame energy, not energy in general.

In the (pc, E) plane the relation $E^2-(pc)^2=(mc^2)^2$ is a hyperbola. A given particle slides along its own hyperbola as you boost frames — E and p both change — yet the curve's apex on the E-axis never moves. That fixed apex is the mass.

03 A crucial distinction

Invariant is not the same as conserved

These two words sound alike and collapse together in everyday speech. In relativity they are different properties, and the difference is the whole reason mass can be created and destroyed.

Invariant

Same numerical value in every reference frame. Mass is invariant: $p^\mu p_\mu$ is a Lorentz scalar.

Conserved

Same value before and after a process, within one frame. Energy and momentum are conserved. The sum of the masses of the parts is generally not.

Consider a particle of mass $M$ at rest decaying into two photons. Total energy and momentum are conserved. The invariant mass of the two-photon system equals $M$ in any frame. But the masses of the products sum to zero:

$$M \;\longrightarrow\; \gamma+\gamma,\qquad m_\gamma+m_\gamma=0\neq M=M_{\text{system}}$$
Mass was not conserved (0 ≠ M), even though it stayed invariant for the system as a whole. Binding energy works the same way in reverse: a bound nucleus weighs less than its free constituents — the mass defect — which is why fusion and fission release energy.

Hold this thought: if a system's mass need not equal the sum of its parts' masses, then we can build mass out of constituents that have none. That is the next section.

04 Mass from massless light

A box of light weighs more

The most literal proof that mass is stored energy: take two photons — each strictly massless — and the system they form has nonzero invariant mass, built from nothing but their energy and geometry.

Give each photon energy $E$. A photon's four-momentum is null, $p^\mu p_\mu=0$. Send the second one off at angle θ from the first:

$$p_1^\mu=\tfrac{E}{c}(1,\,1,\,0),\qquad p_2^\mu=\tfrac{E}{c}(1,\,\cos\theta,\,\sin\theta)$$

Add them and take the invariant norm of the total. Using $\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1$, the algebra collapses neatly:

$$M^2c^2=(p_1+p_2)^\mu(p_1+p_2)_\mu=\Big(\tfrac{2E}{c}\Big)^2-\Big(\tfrac{E}{c}\Big)^2\!\big[(1+\cos\theta)^2+\sin^2\theta\big]$$
$$\boxed{\,Mc^{2}=E\sqrt{2\,(1-\cos\theta)}\,}$$
Antiparallel (θ = 180°): Mc² = 2E — maximum mass from the same total energy. Parallel (θ = 0): M = 0. The mass depends only on how the energy is arranged, never on any "stuff."

05 Where visible mass lives

99% of you is binding energy

Essentially all the mass you can weigh — protons, neutrons, nuclei, you — is not the intrinsic mass of fundamental particles. It is the field energy of the strong interaction, QCD, made concrete by $E_0=mc^2$.

A proton masses about 938 MeV/c². Its three valence quarks contribute almost nothing:

$$2\,m_u+m_d\;\approx\;2(2.2)+4.7\;\approx\;9\ \text{MeV}\;\approx\;1\%$$
The remaining ~99% is the kinetic energy of confined quarks plus the energy stored in the gluon field — the QCD scale Λ_QCD emerging dynamically. This is "mass without mass."

Rigorously, the proton mass is the proton's expectation value of the trace of the stress–energy tensor, and lattice QCD lets us split it into pieces. Toggle between the headline split and the four-term decomposition.

why this is rigorous, not hand-waving

QCD has no mass scale in its classical Lagrangian for massless quarks — yet a proton mass appears. This is dimensional transmutation: quantum effects (the running of the coupling, the trace anomaly) generate the scale Λ_QCD. Lattice simulations compute the 938 MeV from first principles with no input proton mass. The mass is genuinely emergent.

06 Where intrinsic mass comes from

The Higgs sets the 1% — and the electron

The fundamental particles — quarks, charged leptons, the W and Z — have no internal parts, so their rest mass can't be binding energy. It comes from how strongly they couple to the Higgs field, which fills all of space with a constant background value.

The Higgs field has a vacuum expectation value (a nonzero value even in empty space):

$$v\approx 246\ \text{GeV}$$

Each fermion's mass is its Yukawa coupling $y_f$ to that field, times the vev. The electron, having no constituents, gets its 0.511 MeV entirely this way:

$$m_f=\frac{y_f\,v}{\sqrt{2}}$$
A free parameter per particle — the Standard Model does not predict the y_f, it measures them. The top quark has y ≈ 1; the electron has y ≈ 3×10⁻⁶. Twelve orders of magnitude of mass, all from one knob each.

The W and Z are a separate story: they eat the Higgs field's would-be Goldstone bosons (spontaneous symmetry breaking) and acquire mass through the gauge coupling, not a Yukawa:

$$m_W=\tfrac12 g\,v,\qquad m_Z=\tfrac12\sqrt{g^2+g'^2}\,v$$

07 Putting it together

So — is mass just stored energy?

For composite systems, almost exactly. For fundamental particles, no. The honest graduate-level statement holds both at once.

$$m=\frac{E_{\text{rest}}}{c^{2}}\quad\text{always};\qquad \underbrace{E_{\text{rest}}\approx E_{\text{QCD}}}_{\text{composites, }\sim99\%}\;,\qquad \underbrace{m_f=\tfrac{y_f v}{\sqrt2}}_{\text{elementary}}$$

Mass is the invariant rest-frame energy of a system. Where that energy comes from depends on what you're weighing: it is QCD field and kinetic energy for everything composite — which is nearly all visible mass — and Higgs-generated for the handful of truly fundamental particles. The slogan is right about the proton and wrong about the electron, and now you can say exactly why.

one convention flag for your career

Stay with invariant mass throughout. "Relativistic mass" $\gamma m$ is deprecated — say that energy and momentum grow with velocity; mass does not. Every modern paper means the rest mass when it writes $m$.
