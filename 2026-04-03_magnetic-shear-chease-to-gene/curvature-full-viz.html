Magnetic Curvature

How much the field line bends radially at each point — and whether that bending pushes plasma toward the wall or holds it in.

The one-sentence version

A field line curves as it goes around the torus. Curvature κ measures how much it bends radially — toward or away from the plasma center — at each point along the line.

Why only radial bending matters
↔️

Radial = the dangerous direction

Pressure is high in the center and low at the edge. If the field bends outward (toward low pressure), it's like pushing plasma downhill — perturbations grow. If it bends inward, it pushes plasma back uphill — stable.

↕️

Up/down bending = don't care

The field also curves as it follows the flux surface shape vertically. But that doesn't push plasma across the pressure gradient, so it doesn't drive instabilities. GENE only tracks the radial component.

The donut has two sides

Outboard = the outside of the donut, far from the hole. The magnetic field is weaker here and the field lines curve away from the center. This is the bad side.

Inboard = the inside of the donut, close to the hole. The field is stronger and field lines curve toward the center. This is the good side.

Why the outboard side wins
BALL ON HILL VS BALL IN VALLEYANIMATED
💥

Outboard: ball on a hilltop

Curvature and pressure gradient point the same way (both outward). Any tiny perturbation rolls downhill and grows. This is where turbulence is born. It's called "ballooning" because the plasma bulges outward here.

🛡️

Inboard: ball in a valley

Curvature points inward, opposing the pressure gradient. Perturbations get pushed back. This side is inherently stable. But the field line spends less time here (shorter path on the inside of the donut).

The outboard side dominates

A field line spends more time on the outboard side (bigger circumference). So even though the inboard side is stabilizing, the bad curvature region gets more weight overall. That's why tokamak turbulence is mostly driven from the outboard midplane. Plasma shaping (elongation, triangularity) tries to reduce the bad curvature there — try the sliders on the first tab.

Where curvature lives

In CHEASE's equilibrium, curvature exists as a 2D field across the whole R,Z cross-section — every point has a κ value. But GENE only needs it along one field line, so it gets collapsed to a 1D array.

Why GENE cares about curvature
🌊

It drives the turbulence

The turbulence drive term in GENE's equations is literally κ times the pressure gradient. Where κ is positive (outboard), turbulence grows. Where negative (inboard), it's suppressed. Wrong κ = wrong turbulence = wrong transport prediction.

🔄

Particle drifts depend on it

Charged particles in a curved field drift sideways (curvature drift). GENE needs the exact κ profile to get these drifts right. The drift direction flips between inboard and outboard — that's built into the κ sign.
