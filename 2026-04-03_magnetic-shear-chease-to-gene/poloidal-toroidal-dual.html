What you're seeing

Left (Poloidal): You sliced the donut open and are looking at the D-shaped cross-section. Each dot traces its poloidal path — the inner one zips around fast (low q), the outer one crawls (high q). This is where you see the differential rotation that creates shear.

Right (Toroidal): You're looking down at the donut from above. The concentric circles are flux surfaces. The dots spiral around toroidally — they all move at the same toroidal rate, but their radial position oscillates because of the poloidal component. At high shear, the inner and outer dots drift apart in phase.

Bottom (3D): The full picture. Drag to rotate. The colored lines are actual field line helices on the torus surface.

Connecting to shear

Crank the ŝ slider and watch: it changes how much the q values differ between surfaces. At ŝ = 0, all three dots move in sync (same q everywhere). At ŝ = 3, the outer surface has a much higher q and visibly lags behind the inner one on the poloidal view. That growing phase difference is magnetic shear — the thing GENE needs to compute turbulent transport.
