How the two slices relate

Poloidal (left): Slice the donut vertically — like cutting a bagel in half. You see the nested elliptical flux surfaces. Field lines circle around poloidally. The inner one (low q) goes around fast; the outer one (high q) goes slow. This is where you see the shear — different speeds on different surfaces.

Toroidal (right): Slice the donut horizontally through the midplane. You see concentric rings — one per flux surface. All three dots orbit at the exact same toroidal rate, locked together in sync. That's correct! Poloidal motion (where q matters) is vertical, out of this plane — you can't see it from above. The rings stay nested and never cross.

The key insight: q only shows up in the poloidal view. The toroidal view proves the surfaces are properly nested. Compare the two: left panel shows action (different speeds), right panel shows order (everything in lockstep). Shear lives entirely in the poloidal picture.
