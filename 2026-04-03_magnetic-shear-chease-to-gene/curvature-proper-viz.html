Reading the visualization

3D torus (top): The color on the surface IS the curvature. Bright red on the outboard side = strong bad curvature. Green on the inboard = good curvature. The moving dot traces a field line — watch how it passes through red (danger) and green (safety) zones as it spirals.

Cross-section (middle): Actual curved field line segments showing which way they bend. On the right (outboard) the lines curve away from the center — that's the instability. On the left (inboard) they curve toward the center — that's the stabilizing region. The little circles show the "center of curvature" — the point the field line is bending around.

Profile (bottom): κ value as the field line goes around poloidally. Above zero = bad, below = good. The dot moves in sync with the field line above so you can see exactly where you are on the profile.

Try this

Crank elongation up to 2.0 — watch the red region on the outboard side get smaller and less intense. That's why tokamaks are elongated: it reduces bad curvature.

Set triangularity to negative (−0.3) — the cross-section flips to a reversed-D shape, and the curvature distribution changes dramatically. This is the idea behind negative triangularity tokamaks.
