The connection: the ring's local curvature (how tight the bend is) relates to what the metrics look like there. Where the ring bends sharply outward, |B| is weak and g^xx is different. Where it's on the inboard straight section, |B| is strong. And the progressive tilt from shear shows up as g^xy growing with z regardless of the ring shape — that's purely from neighboring surfaces twisting at different rates.

METRIC VALUES ALONG THE RINGSYNCED

Three plots synced with the dot above. Each plot is one metric coefficient going around the ring. The yellow line shows where the dot currently is. Notice:

• g^xx — gentle wave, slightly larger outboard. This is from the flux surfaces being spaced differently.

• g^xy — starts at 0 at outboard, grows linearly. This is pure shear. The ring shape doesn't cause this — neighboring surfaces twisting differently does.

• g^yy — grows quadratically from center. This is shear compounding — further from the midplane, the binormal distance gets more and more stretched.
