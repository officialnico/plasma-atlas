What you're seeing

Top: The full 2D curvature field κ(R,Z) on CHEASE's cross-section grid. Every pixel is a point in the R,Z plane, colored by its local curvature value. Red on the right (outboard) = bad curvature. Green on the left (inboard) = good. The white contour lines are flux surfaces. The bright highlighted ring is your selected surface.

Bottom: The 1D slice extracted by walking around that one surface. This is literally what gets written to the GENE geometry file — κ as a function of poloidal angle θ. The left plot above is the 2D reality, the bottom is the 1D projection GENE actually uses.

Hover over the 2D map to see exact κ values at any point. Drag the ρ slider to pick different surfaces and watch the extracted profile change.

Why this matters

The 2D heatmap makes it obvious that curvature is fundamentally a 2D quantity — it varies across the entire cross-section. CHEASE has to compute it everywhere to solve the equilibrium. But GENE only needs it along one field line, so the handoff is a dimensionality reduction: 2D grid → 1D array. That's the core of the CHEASE-to-GENE interface for curvature.
