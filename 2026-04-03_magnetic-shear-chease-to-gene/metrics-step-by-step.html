STEP-BY-STEP: ONE CELL BEING TRANSFORMED

Step 1: Start with GENE's square. This is what GENE "thinks" the cell looks like — unit square, x and y perpendicular, everything uniform.

Step 2: Apply g^xx — stretch or compress radially (horizontally). The square becomes a rectangle. Wider where flux surfaces are farther apart, narrower where they're closer.

Step 3: Apply g^xy — skew the rectangle. This is shear. The top edge shifts sideways relative to the bottom. The amount of shift is proportional to s-hat × z. At z=0 there's no shift. At z=±π it's maximum. This is the main one.

Step 4: Apply g^yy — stretch binormally (vertically). The parallelogram gets taller. This grows with z² because of shear.

THE FULL TUBE — EACH CELL TRANSFORMED BY ITS LOCAL METRICS

Each column is one cell at a different z-position. The green outlines are GENE's uniform grid. The purple filled shapes are what those cells actually look like after applying the local metrics. In the center (z=0, outboard) they nearly match. Toward the edges (z=±π, inboard) the cells get progressively more tilted and stretched.

This is the entire point of the metrics: they tell GENE "your nice square grid cells are actually these wonky parallelograms in reality, and here's exactly how wonky at each z-position." Without this info, GENE would compute distances and derivatives wrong everywhere except the outboard midplane.
