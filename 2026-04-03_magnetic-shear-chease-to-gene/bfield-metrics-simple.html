|B| and the Metrics

The last pieces of the geometry file. Field strength and coordinate conversion.

|B| - Magnetic Field Strength 1D array along z

|B| = how strong the magnetic field is at each point along the field line. It's stronger on the inboard side (close to the torus hole, small R) and weaker on the outboard side (far from the hole, large R). This is the basic 1/R effect from the toroidal field coils.

|B| along the field line

Particle trapping

Particles bouncing between strong-field and weak-field sides get trapped. They can't make it past the high-|B| inboard region. These trapped particles drive Trapped Electron Modes (TEM) - a major turbulence source. The |B| profile sets the trap depth.

Mirror force

The |B| variation creates a force along the field line. Particles heading toward stronger field get pushed back. GENE needs |B|(z) to compute this correctly.

Grad-B drift

The gradient of |B| causes particles to drift sideways. This combines with the curvature drift to drive turbulence on the outboard side.

Related to curvature

Where |B| is weak (outboard), curvature is bad. Where |B| is strong (inboard), curvature is good. They're correlated but enter GENE's equations differently.

g^xx, g^xy, g^yy - Metric Coefficients 1D arrays along z

GENE works in a field-aligned coordinate system (x, y, z) that is curved and stretched. One unit of x doesn't mean the same real distance everywhere. The metrics are the conversion factors - they tell GENE how to turn coordinate distances into real physical distances at each point along the tube.

Think of a Mercator map: Greenland looks huge but it's actually small. The metrics are like a table saying "at this position, 1cm on the map = X km in reality."

g^xx - radial stretching

How much real radial distance per unit of GENE's x coordinate. Changes because flux surfaces are closer together on the outboard side. Scales the effective radial wavenumber k_x.

g^yy - binormal stretching

Same for the y direction (perpendicular to both field line and radial). Scales the binormal wavenumber k_y. Grows along z because of shear.

g^xy - the cross term - This is where shear physically shows up in the geometry. Because neighboring field lines twist at different rates, the x and y directions aren't perpendicular everywhere. g^xy starts at zero at the outboard midplane and grows linearly with z. This is the geometric version of the twist-and-shift. It makes eddies "feel" like they're at shorter wavelengths away from the midplane, which is how shear stabilizes turbulence.

All three metrics along the field line

The key equation: k_perp² = g^xx·k_x² + 2·g^xy·k_x·k_y + g^yy·k_y². This is the effective perpendicular wavenumber at each z-point. Every spatial derivative GENE computes uses these metrics. Wrong metrics = wrong mode structure = wrong turbulence.

The Complete Geometry File everything GENE gets

Two single numbers: q = 2.50 and s-hat = 1.85. Plus six 1D arrays along z (~128 points each). That's it. This is everything GENE knows about the magnetic field shape.
