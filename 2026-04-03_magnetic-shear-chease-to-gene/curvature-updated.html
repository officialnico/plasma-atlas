Magnetic Curvature

How much the field line bends radially — toward or away from the plasma center — at each point as it goes around the surface.

The idea

Field lines in a tokamak curve as they spiral around. Curvature κ measures how much a field line bends radially at each point — toward the plasma center or away from it. Only the radial component matters because that's the direction of the pressure gradient.

Radial = the dangerous direction

Pressure is high at the center, low at the edge. Bending outward (toward low pressure) is unstable — like a ball on a hilltop. Bending inward is stable — ball in a valley. Up/down bending just follows the flux surface shape and doesn't matter for stability.

It changes around the surface

Unlike shear (same everywhere on a surface), curvature flips sign as you go around. Positive on the outboard side (bad), negative on the inboard side (good). That's why it's indexed by θ — you need ~128 values to describe it, not just one.

Outboard vs inboard

Outboard = outside of the donut, far from the hole. Field is weaker, bends outward. Bad — drives turbulence.

Inboard = inside of the donut, near the hole. Field is stronger, bends inward. Good — stabilizes.

BALL ANALOGY

Outboard: ball on a hilltop

κ and ∇p point the same way. Perturbations roll downhill and grow. This is where turbulence starts — "ballooning" because the plasma bulges outward.

Inboard: ball in a valley

κ opposes ∇p. Perturbations get pushed back. But the field line spends less time here (shorter path on the inside of the donut), so it only partially cancels the bad side.

Shaping helps

Higher elongation reduces outboard curvature. Negative triangularity reshapes where the bad curvature sits. Try the sliders on the first tab — watch the arrows shrink on the outboard side as you crank elongation up.

How CHEASE computes curvature

CHEASE doesn't store curvature as a 2D heatmap on its R,Z grid. It works surface by surface. For each flux surface, it traces around poloidally and computes κ(θ) — one profile per surface. Each profile is a 1D array.

κ drives the turbulence

GENE's turbulence equation has a term that's literally κ × pressure gradient. Positive κ (outboard) = energy source for turbulence. Negative κ (inboard) = stabilizing. The balance between them determines how much heat escapes.

Particle drifts need it

Charged particles in a curved field drift sideways. The drift speed and direction depend on κ. Wrong curvature = wrong drifts = wrong transport.
