What you're seeing

Left (Poloidal): Slice the donut to see the cross-section. Each field line traces around its own flux surface ring — inner (low q) races around fast, outer (high q) goes slow. They never cross because each stays on its own surface.

Right (Unrolled Tube): Imagine cutting one flux surface tube and unrolling it flat. The x-axis is toroidal angle (how far around the long way), y-axis is poloidal angle (short way). A field line is a straight diagonal — its slope = 1/q. Steeper = lower q = tighter spiral. Three tubes are stacked so you can see the slopes differ.

Bottom (3D): The actual helical field lines on the torus. Drag to rotate.

Why the old toroidal view was wrong

Looking down at a torus from above, all field lines on all surfaces just go around in circles at the same rate — you can't see the poloidal twist from above. The previous view was faking a radial wobble that would imply field lines crossing between surfaces, which can't happen in a good equilibrium. The unrolled tube view actually shows you the different twist rates properly.
