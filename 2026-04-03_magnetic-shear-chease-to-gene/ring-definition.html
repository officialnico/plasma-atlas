STEP 1: CHEASE SOLVES THE EQUILIBRIUMpsi(R,Z) CONTOURS

CHEASE solves the Grad-Shafranov equation and gets ψ(R,Z) everywhere on the grid. The contour lines are surfaces of constant ψ — those are your flux surfaces. The highlighted one is at your selected ρ. Notice it's not a circle — it's elongated (taller than wide) and has triangularity (D-shaped, pointier on one side).

Drag ρ and watch: inner surfaces (low ρ) are nearly circular. Outer surfaces (high ρ) are more D-shaped. The shape comes from the equilibrium physics, not from any formula we impose.

↓
STEP 2: WALK AROUND THE RING — SAMPLE (R,Z) POINTSANIMATED

CHEASE picks your surface and walks around it, placing sample points at equal θ intervals. Each dot is an (R,Z) coordinate on the ring. The number next to each dot is its z-index — this becomes the z coordinate in GENE's arrays.

The dots are denser where the surface curves sharply and sparser where it's gentle. The moving dot traces the path CHEASE follows — starting at the outboard midplane (right), going up, around the inboard (left), back down, and returning.

↓
STEP 3: AT EACH POINT, COMPUTE THE GEOMETRYALL ARRAYS

At each sample point, CHEASE computes κ, |B|, g^xx, g^xy, g^yy, and Jacobian. The moving dot on the cross-section is synced with the vertical line on the plots — you can see which point on the ring produces which values.

This is the whole pipeline. The D-shaped ring → sample points → compute values at each point → write out the arrays. GENE reads these arrays and never sees the (R,Z) coordinates or the ring shape directly.
