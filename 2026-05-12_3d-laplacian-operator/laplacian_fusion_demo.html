What is ∇²?

The Laplacian is a single scalar that asks one question at every point in a field:

"Is this point higher or lower than the average of the points right next to it?"

∇²φ = ∂²φ/∂x² + ∂²φ/∂y² + ∂²φ/∂z²

It is the sum of second derivatives — a measure of how much a value at a point differs from the local average around it.

Positive ∇² → the point sits in a dip, neighbors are higher, stuff flows in.
Negative ∇² → the point is a peak, neighbors are lower, stuff flows out.
Zero ∇² → perfectly balanced with neighborhood.

The wave equation

∂²u/∂t² = c² ∇²u

This is what the surface in front of you is solving. Every point's acceleration is proportional to its Laplacian. Peaks pull themselves down, troughs push themselves up — that's a wave.

Why fusion cares

plasma A fusion reactor holds a 100-million-degree plasma. Three equations rule it, and ∇² appears in all three:

∂n/∂t = D ∇²n − losses
particle density diffusion
∂T/∂t = χ ∇²T + heating
heat transport
∇²A = −μ₀ J
magnetic confinement (Poisson)

The sign and magnitude of ∇² tells engineers whether heat is leaking out of the core faster than fusion creates it. If ∇²T is too negative at the edge, the plasma cools and dies. If the magnetic ∇²A doesn't shape the field correctly, the plasma escapes and the reaction stops.

A tokamak is, in one sentence, a machine built to make the Laplacian of pressure point inward everywhere at once.
