How the molecule acts

It doesn’t scrub your skin. It rewrites the instructions a skin cell reads.

Tretinoin is the acid form of vitamin A. It’s already active, so it can walk straight into a skin cell’s nucleus and switch genes on and off. Everything else — the peeling, the clearer pores, the smoother texture — follows from that one move.

What the rewired cells do

↑ TURNOVER

Faster cell renewal

Keratinocytes proliferate and mature more quickly, so the surface renews on a shorter clock.

↓ PLUGGING

Normalized pore lining

Cells lining the follicle stop clumping and sticking, so plugs (microcomedones) don’t form. This is the core acne effect.

↑ COLLAGEN

More collagen, less breakdown

Boosts type I & III collagen and blocks the MMP enzymes that degrade it — smoother texture, softer fine lines.

↓ PIGMENT

Dispersed melanin

Quicker turnover plus effects on melanocytes break up clustered pigment, fading dark spots over time.

Why it gets worse before it gets better

The purge is your backlog, surfacing all at once.

Under your skin right now sits a pipeline of microcomedones — invisible plugs, weeks away from ever becoming spots. The purge in three beats

01

The pipeline exists

Acne-prone skin is always forming microcomedones — tiny plugs of dead cells and oil, sitting at different depths, weeks or months from ever showing.

02

Turnover rushes them up

Tretinoin speeds cell renewal, so the whole backlog is pushed toward the surface at once. That compressed surge is the purge — usually peaking around weeks 2–6.

03

Then the well runs dry

Once the backlog clears and the pore lining is normalized, fewer new plugs form. Breakouts settle — typically below where you started — by roughly weeks 8–12.

Purging vs. a reaction — not the same thing

Looks like purging

  • Breakouts in the spots where you already tend to break out
  • The usual kind of blemishes, just more at once
  • Peaks early, then clearly eases within about 8–12 weeks

Looks like irritation

  • Redness, stinging, burning, or peeling in new areas
  • Breakouts where you don’t normally get them
  • Keeps escalating instead of settling — a cue to slow down or check in with a professional
