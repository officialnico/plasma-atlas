What you're seeing

The donut shape is a flux surface — one shell out of many nested shells. Each blue line is a magnetic field line, spiraling helically: it goes the long way around (toroidal) while also winding the short way (poloidal). After many laps, a single field line traces out the entire surface.

The orange dot rides along one field line. At its location, you can see the three coordinate directions: x points outward through the surface, z points along B (tangent to the spiral), and y is what's left — perpendicular to both, lying in the surface.

The safety factor q tells you the pitch: q ≈ 3 here, meaning the field line goes 3 times around the long way for every 1 time around the short way. In real tokamaks q varies from about 1 in the core to 3–5 at the edge, and that variation is what creates the magnetic shear that stabilizes turbulence.

Notice that there's no preferred starting point on the surface — every field line is equivalent up to rotation. This symmetry is why z (along B) is well-defined globally even though the geometry is curved: the field tells you which way to go at every point.
