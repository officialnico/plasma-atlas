What do (x, y, z) actually mean?

Drag the sliders. Watch the dot. The numbers are a recipe for getting to a point.

Think of (x, y, z) as a three-step recipe for finding a point in the torus:

Move the sliders to set x, y, z — see where you end up
drag · scroll
1.x picks which flux surface (which donut shell)
2.y picks which field line on that shell
3.z says how far along that field line to walk
x 0.50
y 0.00
z 0.00
What you should try

Move only x: the dot jumps between donut shells, in and out. Different x = different flux surface.

Move only y: the dot slides around the surface perpendicular to the field. Different y = different field line.

Move only z: the dot slides along the highlighted field line, spiraling helically around the torus.

So the y value is just "how far along the binormal direction did I step from the reference field line." It's a length, in meters. y = 0 means you're on the reference (highlighted) field line. y = 0.5 m means you stepped half a meter along the binormal — and now you're on a different field line, one that's parallel to the reference but offset.

The reason this is confusing: in everyday geometry, if you walk "1 meter east" you always end up at the same offset, no matter where you started. Here, "1 meter along y" lands you on a different field line every time, because y is a direction defined locally — it depends on where the field is pointing right there.
