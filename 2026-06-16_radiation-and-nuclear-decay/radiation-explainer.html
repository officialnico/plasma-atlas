Field Guide · From the neutron conversation

Radiation is just energy on the move.

It travels two ways: as waves of pure energy (light, X-rays, gamma rays) or as tiny pieces of matter flying fast (the alpha, beta, and neutron particles). Everything dangerous — and most things useful — comes down to how much energy each one carries. Scroll through and watch it happen.

01 / THE FAMILY TREETwo ways energy travels

Every kind of radiation belongs to one of these two branches.

〰️

Electromagnetic

Ripples in electric & magnetic fields — no mass, always moving at the speed of light. This is light itself, plus radio, microwaves, X-rays and gamma rays.

radio · micro · infrared · visible · UV · x-ray · gamma
⦿

Particle

Actual chunks of matter — bits of atoms — hurled outward at high speed. They have mass, and some carry electric charge.

alpha · beta · neutron · proton · cosmic rays

02 / YOUR QUESTIONSo are X-rays waves or particles?

Both. This is the famous trick of quantum mechanics. The same X-ray is correctly described as a smooth wave and as a stream of energy packets called photons. Neither picture is "more true" — you pick whichever explains what you're looking at. Flip the switch and watch the same beam change costume.

The wave view explains how X-rays diffract, bend, and form images. ↑

04 / THE DANGER LINEWhat makes radiation harmful

One idea separates "warms your soup" from "wrecks your DNA": can a single photon kick an electron clean off an atom? That's ionization. Below the threshold the atom just jiggles (heat). Above it, bonds break and DNA tears. Drag the energy up and watch the electron decide.

photon energy: 3.0 eV threshold ≈ 10 eV

05 / THE PARTICLESThe matter that flies at you

The other branch. These are real fragments of atoms — including the neutron from your reactor questions.

Alpha α

2 protons + 2 neutrons · charge +2

Big and slow. A helium nucleus. Brutal up close but stopped by skin — only dangerous if you breathe or swallow it.

mass: heavycharge: +2

Beta β

a single electron · charge −1

A fast electron spat out by a decaying nucleus. Light and quick; slips through skin but not through a sheet of aluminum.

mass: tinycharge: −1

Gamma / X-ray γ

a photon · no mass · no charge

Pure energy from branch one, but lethal at the high end. No charge means nothing slows it easily — needs thick lead or concrete.

mass: 0charge: 0

Neutron n

a bare neutron · no charge

Heavy and uncharged — a rare combo. It ignores electrons entirely and only stops by slamming into a nucleus. That's what makes it so penetrating.

mass: heavycharge: 0

06 / STOPPING ITWhat blocks what

Each type is halted by something different. Watch them launch from the left and hit their wall.

The neutron twist — Notice the neutron sails straight through lead, the very thing that stops gamma rays. With no charge, it doesn't "see" the dense electrons in metal. It's stopped by water, plastic, or concrete — materials packed with hydrogen. Why hydrogen? A neutron and a proton weigh almost the same, so a head-on bounce drains its energy fast — exactly the recoil-proton mechanism that makes neutrons so damaging to your (very watery) body.
