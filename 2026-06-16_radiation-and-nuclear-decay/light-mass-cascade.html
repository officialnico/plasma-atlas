Animated · three answers, in motion

A finite burst, a spectrum, and missing mass.

Made visible: a nucleus emits a finite cascade of gammas and goes quiet — and its ladder has rungs at all only because it's a confined quantum system; the only truly massless particles sit at one end of a vast mass spectrum; and a proton's weight is almost all binding energy, not quark mass. Watch each one run.

01 / FINITE, NOT INFINITEThe gamma cascade runs out

An excited nucleus drops down its discrete energy levels, emitting one photon per step — each carrying exactly that step's energy. When it hits the ground state it has nothing left to give and goes silent, until something pumps energy back in.

Colour here is a code, not reality — a real γ is invisible, far past violet. Brighter = higher energy, so each photon's shade now matches its MeV; the nucleus is left neutral silver so orange can keep meaning "alpha" in the other guides.

So: not infinite. The total is capped by the starting excitation energy (a few MeV), split into a handful of photons. The gammas aren't stored — each is created at the moment of a downward jump. Reach the bottom and the well is dry.

02 / WHY RUNGS AT ALLWhy the ladder is discrete

The rungs aren't a drawing convenience — quantum mechanics forces them. A nucleon trapped in the nucleus behaves like a wave, and a wave confined to a finite space can only take shapes that fit the boundary, exactly like the harmonics of a guitar string pinned at both ends. The patterns that fit form a discrete set; the ones in between simply can't exist. Watch which waves are allowed.

Confined + wavelike → discrete. A free particle can have any energy at all (a continuous ramp); only when a wave is bound in a well do the allowed energies snap to a ladder — the same reason atoms emit discrete colours and a string plays discrete notes. (This is the simplest case, the infinite square well, where energy grows as n²; real nuclei use the shell model, but the principle is identical.) Those discrete rungs are exactly why the gammas in panel 01 come out at sharp, specific energies.

03 / THE MASSLESS ENDEvery particle's mass, on one scale

A logarithmic mass axis spanning twelve orders of magnitude. The photon and gluon sit at true zero (far left). The neutrino is almost there — but not massless. Everything else climbs to the colossal top quark.

bosons leptons quarks precisely known
Massless club: photon and gluon (and the hypothetical graviton). Neutrinos are millions of times lighter than an electron but have a tiny nonzero mass — proven by their flavour oscillation. The electron's mass is a crisp dot (known to ~10 digits); the light quarks get fuzzy bands, because they can't be isolated and their mass is scale-dependent.

04 / WHERE MASS COMES FROMA proton is mostly energy

Do we know quark masses? Roughly — and it barely matters for a proton's weight, because the quarks' rest mass is a rounding error. Watch what actually fills a proton's 938 MeV/c².

~1% vs ~99%. The up + down quark rest masses sum to only ~9 MeV — about 1% of the proton. The other ~99% is the energy of the churning gluon field, turned into mass by E = mc². So the electron's mass we know exactly; a quark's "mass" is model- and scale-dependent (current vs constituent mass); and the proton's mass is mostly not "mass" at all — it's bound energy.

LIGHT, MASS & THE GAMMA CASCADE

Three answers in motion: the photon burst is finite because energy levels are discrete and bounded; massless means exactly zero rest mass — a club of two confirmed members; and most of the mass you're made of is the binding energy holding your quarks together, not the quarks themselves.
